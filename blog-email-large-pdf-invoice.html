How to Email a Large PDF Invoice Without It Bouncing Back

If you've ever hit send on an invoice email and gotten an instant bounce-back, you've run into an attachment size limit. Most providers cap attachments somewhere between 20-25MB — Gmail sits at 25MB, Outlook at 20MB — and a PDF full of scanned pages or high-resolution images can blow past that fast.

Why invoice PDFs get so large

It's almost never the text causing the problem. The usual culprits are scanned pages saved at unnecessarily high resolution, embedded fonts duplicated across every page, or photos of receipts pasted in at full camera resolution. A 3-page text invoice should be well under 1MB — if yours is 20MB+, something in it is bloated.

The fastest fix: compress it first

Before troubleshooting anything else, run the file through a PDF compressor. It strips out duplicate fonts, unused embedded data, and formatting bloat without touching what the file actually looks like. For text-heavy invoices, this alone often solves the problem in one step.

If it's still too big

  • Check for embedded high-res images. If the invoice includes scanned receipts or photos, those are usually the real weight. Re-scanning at a lower resolution before combining, or converting to JPG and re-embedding at a smaller size, can cut file size dramatically.
  • Split it into parts. If it's a long multi-invoice statement, use a PDF splitter to send only the relevant pages instead of the whole bundle.
  • Use a cloud link instead of an attachment. For genuinely large files (large multi-year statements, image-heavy reports), upload to Google Drive or Dropbox and share a link rather than attaching the file directly.

A quick pre-send checklist

  1. Compress the PDF first — it's free and takes seconds.
  2. Check the resulting file size before attaching.
  3. If still over your provider's limit, split or convert oversized images.
  4. For recurring large files, consider a shared drive link as your default instead of attachments.
