How to Compress a PDF Under 5MB for Email or Upload

Some upload portals — government forms, certain client intake systems, older CRM software — cap files at 5MB, well below the 20-25MB most email providers allow. Getting there reliably takes a slightly different approach than just "compress and hope."

Start with a basic compress pass

Run the file through a PDF compressor first. For text-based documents — contracts, invoices, typed reports — this alone often gets you well under 5MB, since most of the excess size in those files is structural bloat, not actual content.

If it's still over 5MB after compressing

At that point, the file's size is very likely genuine image data — scanned pages or embedded photos — which a structural compressor can't shrink further without changing the images themselves. Two practical options:

  • Split the document. If the portal allows multiple uploads, split the PDF into smaller sections instead of forcing one file under the limit.
  • Re-scan at a lower resolution. If you control the original scan, 200 DPI is plenty for most business documents — 600 DPI (common on default scanner settings) produces files 5-10x larger for no real benefit on a typical form or invoice.

A quick gut-check before you scan or photograph anything

If you're about to create a new PDF from a scan or phone photo and know it needs to land under a tight size limit, save yourself a step: scan at 200 DPI or use a phone photo through Receipt to PDF rather than a maximum-resolution scanner setting. It's much easier to start small than to compress your way down after the fact.

Try it: Compress a PDF free, or split a large one into smaller files.
